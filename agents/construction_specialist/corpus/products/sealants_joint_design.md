## 1. Sealant chemistries (and when each fits)

- **Silicone** — excellent weathering: UV, ozone, oxygen resistance and good low-temperature flexibility; high movement capability. Trade-offs: generally **cannot be painted/coated**, lower tear strength, can pick up dirt and can stain porous stone (use non-staining/low-modulus formulations on stone). Common on glass/metal curtain wall and weatherseal joints [JVS Building Services; sealant-chemistry technical summaries].
- **Polyurethane (PU)** — strong mechanical/rheological properties, good adhesion to many substrates, paintable, good abrasion resistance (useful in traffic/horizontal joints). Trade-off: **can chalk, crack, or yellow under prolonged UV** versus silicone [silyl-terminated-polyurethane / construction-sealant literature].
- **STPE / silyl-terminated polyether (a.k.a. MS/SMP "hybrid")** — engineered to combine silicone's weathering with polyurethane's strength/adhesion: paintable, low-stain, bonds to most construction substrates (masonry, stone, concrete, stucco, fiber cement, wood, vinyl), fast cure. Widely used for facade weatherproofing joints [Sto Corp "STPE Sealants"; OSi silyl-terminated polymer literature].
- **Polysulfide** and others exist for specific chemical/immersion exposures — product-specific; **TDS governs.**

> Agent rule: chemistry choice follows the substrate, the movement, the exposure, and whether it must be painted/non-staining — then the **specific product's TDS** governs the rest.

## 2. Sealant classification — ASTM C920

The primary performance spec for elastomeric building-construction sealants is **ASTM C920** ("Standard Specification for Elastomeric Joint Sealants"; current C920-18, reapproved as C920-18(2024)). It classifies sealants in four parts — **Type · Grade · Class · Use** [ASTM C920; Pecora TB-45 summary]:

- **Type:** S = single-component, M = multicomponent.
- **Grade:** P = pourable/self-leveling (horizontal joints), NS = non-sag/gunnable (vertical joints).
- **Class (movement capability):** e.g., 100/50, 50, 35, 25, 12.5 — the ± percent of joint movement the sealant can sustain. **Movement capability is the single most important selection number: a joint that moves more than the sealant's class can stretch will tear.** *(The class designations are established by cyclic-movement testing — confirm the exact referenced test method, commonly ASTM C719, against the standard.)*
- **Use:** application designations (e.g., T traffic, NT non-traffic, I immersion, M mortar/masonry, G glass, A aluminum, O other).

## 3. Joint design — ASTM C1193

**ASTM C1193** ("Standard Guide for Use of Joint Sealants") governs joint design and application — materials (sealant, backing, primer), substrate cleaning/priming, and joint geometry [ASTM C1193].

- **Width-to-depth ratio** (per C1193 guidance as commonly summarized — confirm exact values against the current edition):
  - Joints up to 1/2 in. wide: sealant depth ≈ equal to width (≈1:1).
  - Joints 1/2 in. to 1 in. wide: depth ≈ 1/2 the width, but **not less than 1/4 in.**
  - Joints over 1 in. wide: depth ≈ 1/2 in. regardless of width.
- **Backer rod** sets the depth, prevents **three-sided adhesion**, and gives the sealant the **hourglass profile** it needs to stretch. Closed-cell for most applications; open-cell only for deep joints with no water exposure; bi-cellular where moisture could be trapped.
- **Backer rod OR bond-breaker tape is mandatory on every joint** to prevent three-sided adhesion (sealant must bond to only the two joint faces, not the back). *(Standard practice per C1193.)*

## 4. Substrate prep, priming, compatibility

- Joints must be **clean, dry, and sound**; remove old sealant, dust, laitance, coatings. **Prime where the TDS requires** (priming need is substrate- and product-specific).
- **Compatibility matters:** sealant must be compatible with the substrate (staining of porous stone is a real risk with some silicones) and with adjacent materials/coatings. Manufacturer adhesion/compatibility testing (and field adhesion testing) is the way to confirm — **TDS and project mock-up govern.**

## 5. Facade relevance (LL11 / FISP)

Sealant joints are a primary water-management line on masonry, stone, precast, and curtain-wall facades — perimeter joints at openings, control/expansion joints, and material-transition joints. Failed sealant is a common FISP finding because water intrusion drives the masonry/steel deterioration behind it. Re-sealing is frequently part of facade restoration scope; matching movement class to actual joint movement (§2) is what makes the repair last.

## 6. Common failure modes

- **Adhesive failure** — sealant releases from the substrate face (bad prep, missing primer, incompatible substrate).
- **Cohesive failure** — sealant tears within itself (movement exceeded its class — §2 — or wrong product).
- **Three-sided adhesion** — sealant bonded to the joint back (missing backer rod/bond breaker), so it can't stretch and tears.
- **Wrong width-to-depth / over-deep sealant** — poor stretch geometry, early failure.
- **Staining / incompatibility** — some silicones stain porous stone; always verify on the actual substrate.

## 7. Governing standards & references
- **ASTM C920** — elastomeric joint sealant spec (Type/Grade/Class/Use).
- **ASTM C1193** — guide for use/design of joint sealants (geometry, backing, priming).
- Cyclic-movement test underlying C920 movement classes — **confirm designation (commonly ASTM C719).**
- Manufacturer TDS — controls movement class, joint-size range, primer, substrate list (always controlling).
